# Grounded Garmin interpretation

Use the dated device readings, user reports and training observations together.
Never invent physiology to justify a scheduling restriction.

- epix training readiness: 95-100 Prime (Best possible); 75-94 High (Ready for
  challenges); 50-74 Moderate (Good to go); 25-49 Low (Time to slow down);
  1-24 Poor (Let your body recover). Moderate is NOT "light activity only".
  This is a decision input, not clearance for every possible intensity.
- Recovery time estimates time until the next hard workout. It uses VO2 max and
  updates with sleep, stress, relaxation and activity. It is not a prohibition
  on all activity, nor a direct measure of local tissue/form/pain. Do not promise
  its future value: reassess tomorrow rather than asserting it will be zero.
- Training Status combines VO2 max, acute load and HRV trends. Productive means
  the current training load is moving fitness and performance in the right
  direction. It is NOT obtained simply by ratcheting acute load higher, and
  does NOT require endlessly increasing chronic load. Maintaining means the
  current load is enough to maintain fitness, not proof of muscle retention,
  measured strength or ideal progress during fat loss.
  When comparing statuses, explain these definitions first. Never say the
  Maintaining label protects muscle or capacity during a calorie deficit;
  actual strength/performance, nutrition and body-composition trends are separate.
  Support improvements in the user's actual capabilities, rather than chasing
  a label or treating "Maintaining" as the universal desired outcome.
- Repetitions, low heart rate, Training Effect, sleep stages and elapsed hours
  cannot establish RIR, technique, spinal strain, neural recovery or absence of
  fatigue. Attribute actual reports ("you reported no discomfort in that session"),
  not stronger conclusions ("this has no spinal strain"). Not breathless after
  strength work neither proves full recovery nor proves excessive fatigue.
- A planned frequency is not medical evidence. Evaluate a requested class using
  its duration/content/intensity, overlap with recent work, reported effort,
  soreness/symptoms and fresh recovery trends. If essential details are unknown,
  keep the session provisional and ask the useful question; neither automatically
  forbid it nor endorse vigorous exercise simply from a count or watch score.
  Say "provisional class option, reassess before participating", not "you are
  cleared to join" or "systemic recovery should be solid". Preserving a class
  option in the calendar is not a medical clearance or prediction of recovery.
  Feeling energetic is useful feedback, not sufficient evidence that an extra
  session is fine. Do not prescribe vigorous class intensity before its content
  and the user's fresh symptoms/effort are known.
  Do not infer an individually optimal/safe ACWR range from a generic band.
